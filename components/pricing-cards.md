# Pricing Cards

A pricing section should help people pick a plan quickly and nudge them toward the recommended one.

## When to use

- Designing a pricing page or section.
- Reviewing plan cards, a plan comparison or a subscription picker.

General card rules are in `cards-and-imagery`. Grouping and emphasis laws are in `ux-laws` (Proximity, Von Restorff, Hick). Button hierarchy is in `buttons-and-ctas`.

## Cheat sheet

1. **Favor annual billing.** A Monthly / Annual toggle with a visible discount tag (for example "25% Discount"), annual selected or promoted.
2. **Show the most popular option as the anchor.** The recommended card (usually the middle one) is elevated, filled with the brand color and carries a "Most popular" badge. The others stay outlined or neutral. If everything is emphasised, nothing is.
3. **Name each plan** clearly (Free, Professional, Company).
4. **Evolving illustrations.** Use a plan icon or illustration that grows richer from tier to tier.
5. **Short plan description** under the plan name.
6. **Price large, unit small.** Show the price big with the billing unit ("/month") small beside it, and billing details (billing annually, minimum seats) directly beneath.
7. **Customized CTA per plan.** Different labels and treatments: a tinted "Get started for free", a white-on-blue "Get started" on the featured card, a neutral "Request Demo" for the enterprise tier.
8. **Don't repeat the benefits.** The higher plan starts with "All in the Free plan" and lists only what is new.
9. **Only the most important benefits** in the card; put the full comparison list below the section.
10. **Enterprise tier** can replace the price with a conversation prompt ("Let's chat!").
11. Use check icons for the feature list and a clear structure: tag, plan name and badge grouped in the header.

## Checklist

- [ ] One highlighted recommended plan
- [ ] Annual/monthly toggle with a discount shown
- [ ] Price and billing note adjacent
- [ ] Each plan has its own specific CTA label
- [ ] Feature lists short, not repeated across tiers
