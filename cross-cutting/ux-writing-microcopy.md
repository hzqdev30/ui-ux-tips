# UX Writing and Microcopy

The words in an interface are part of the design. Copy is critical for conversion, trust and a consistent brand voice.

## When to use

- Writing or reviewing any interface text (buttons, errors, prompts, labels, empty states).
- Auditing a design for leftover placeholder or template text.
- Writing cancellation, unsubscribe or "are you sure" flows.

Visual button rules are in `buttons-and-ctas`; field-level error placement is in `forms-and-inputs`; settings label rewrites in `settings-and-profile`.

## 1. Be clear and plain

- Use understandable, jargon-free language organised in a logical journey.
- Say what an action does. Prefer verb plus object ("Create account", "Subscribe") over vague words ("Submit", "OK", "Click here").
- Keep nav labels and button text short.
- Use a voice that fits the audience, consistently across the product.

## 2. Errors do not blame the user

- Do not write "Invalid password". Say what is needed: "Password must be at least 8 characters. Include a number and a capital letter."
- Name the field and explain how to fix it. Avoid generic messages such as "Incorrect data. Please check your info."
- Say what happened and what the user can do next.

## 3. Lead with the benefit

- Prompts should explain the value: "Enable notifications" becomes "Get instant payment notifications!" with a line explaining why.
- Explain why you ask for sensitive information.

## 4. Conversational tone

- Make setup feel like a conversation: reuse the person's name and answers; playful, human wording where it suits the brand. (Flow design: `onboarding-flows`.)
- Empty and error states can be friendly and helpful rather than cold ("Basket is empty").

## 5. No dark patterns

- Do not shame users who leave or decline. A cancellation message like "We guess you don't want to grow as a professional" is manipulative.
- Use a neutral, empathetic message such as "We are sorry to see you leaving", state clearly what changes, and offer an optional feedback link.
- Make destructive options honest and visible; confirm with clear labels ("Yes, delete" / "No, keep it").

## 6. Consistency and honesty

- Keep casing, price format and wording consistent (do not mix "Add to cart", "ADD TO CART", "Add").
- Make the offer wording consistent across screens (do not call access a free gift on one screen and a trial-then-charge on the next).

## 7. Real content early

- Use realistic copy in designs instead of lorem ipsum so layout decisions reflect real length and tone.
- Before sharing a file, search for leftover template text ("Title", "Button", "Text").

## Checklist

- [ ] Labels say what happens
- [ ] Errors explain the fix without blame
- [ ] Prompts state the benefit
- [ ] No shaming or manipulative wording
- [ ] Wording and formats consistent
- [ ] Real copy, no leftover placeholders
